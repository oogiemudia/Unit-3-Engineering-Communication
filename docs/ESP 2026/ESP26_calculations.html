Calculations for the preferred design (Design A): beam and leg seat set

1. Assumptions

  • Sizes and masses are taken from the CAD model (models/esp2026.py): every one of the 39 parts is modelled with its own material density. One set has 128 parts.
  • Life vests are supplied and replaced by the airline (they are cabin safety equipment), so they are left out of the 8 kg seat limit. They are listed with them included as well.
  • Seat pitch 31 in (787 mm), typical for economy. A standard passenger is 77 kg (CS 25.562). Forward emergency landing load 9 g (CS 25.561), checked as a static load on the legs.
  • Labour £15 an hour (brief). Material prices, labour times and bought in prices are estimates from supplier catalogues for a batch of 100 sets; they are listed in section 8.
  • Tooling (moulds, jigs) £60,000 and certification testing (16 g sled tests, flammability) £150,000 are shared over the 100 sets.
  • Cabin 18 to 27 °C: aluminium, carbon fibre and the polymers chosen are all stable well beyond this range, so no thermal allowance is needed.
  • “Sustainable” means recycled content, bio based, or recycled at end of life through an established route (aluminium, steel, thermoplastics, wool and PET).

6. Strength: the legs in a 9 g emergency landing

Forward load on the set: 9 × 9.81 × (3 × 77 + 23.5) = 22,470 N, shared by two legs: 11,235 N a leg, half at each beam bore. The leg is treated as a pin jointed truss (A, B feet; C, D beam bores). Joint equilibrium gives the member forces; stress = force / (strut width × 10 mm plate). Foot reactions: front 6856 N back, 6303 N up; rear 4379 N back, 6303 N down (the rear foot is pulled up).
