Calculations for the preferred design (Design A): solar powered harbour buoy

1. Assumptions

  • Sizes and masses come from the CAD model (models/esp2024.py): all 43 parts are modelled with their own material density; the buoy has 77 parts and the sinker 5.
  • Sea water 1025 kg/m³. The harbour lane is 5 m deep at high water; the buoy carries the weight of the chain hanging from it, about one depth of 10 mm chain (2.2 kg/m in air, 9.6 kg in water). Chain length 3 × depth.
  • Wind for the heel check 17 m/s (gale force 8); tidal current 1.5 m/s; drag coefficient 1 for the float, panels and topmark.
  • Sun: 1 kWh/m² a day in December and 3 in June on an upright panel facing the sun at 50° north; panels lose 25 % to salt, dirt, heat and the charger.
  • Labour £17 an hour (marine fabrication). Material and bought in prices are supplier estimates for a batch of 100; they are listed in section 9.
  • Marine growth, ice and a boat tying up to the buoy are not in the floating calculation; the reserve buoyancy covers them.

The supply is 12 V nominal (12.8 V LiFePO4, 10 to 14.4 V), which suits the lantern, tracker and load pin amplifier. Upright panels collect more in winter, when the sun is low, than flat ones, shed rain, spray and bird droppings, and cannot be stood on. The charger switches the lantern on at dusk from the panel voltage, so there is no separate light sensor to fail.

The steady pull in a gale with the tide (738 N) is less than 2 % of the design load. It is mostly sideways: with the chain 3 times the depth it leaves the buoy at a shallow angle, and even if the strongest tide lifts all the chain off the bed the buoy carries only 188 N more, against 504 N of reserve buoyancy: it dips about 95 mm but stays afloat. Even at the full breaking load of the chain the mooring eye stays below yield: the chain fails first, the load pin sees the pull fall to zero and the alert is sent. Above the hole the FEA (20.8 MPa) agrees with the hand value (22.2 MPa). Beside the hole the FEA is about half the hand value: like Inventor, it spreads the force round the whole hole, so half of it goes straight into the plate above. The real pin bears on the bottom of the hole, so the hand value is the one to design to, and it is well below yield. The FEA peak at the edge of the hole is the stress concentration (about 2 to 3 times the net stress).

  • No steel that can rust: the stainless is 316L (with molybdenum, for sea water), the fasteners A4 (316), the float, topmark and fender polyethylene and rubber, the ballast and sinker concrete.
  • The zinc anode (22) is wired to the stainless tail and the galvanised chain: zinc is more reactive, so it corrodes instead of them. It is changed at the yearly service.
  • Nylon washers and sleeves keep the galvanised chain off the stainless eye where they are not joined through the anode, and all stainless parts are passivated after welding.
